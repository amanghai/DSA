CardCraft: digital wedding invitations for Indian weddings

A complete breakdown of the site's product, pricing, payment, delivery, design system, technology, marketing and the company behind it. Each claim is marked as either a Fact read directly from the site, its code or a public record, or a Read, which is my interpretation.

FactThe pitch is "A digital invitation your guests will keep." The flow is: pick a design, type two names and a date, watch a watermarked preview, pay, then add photos, ceremonies and venues, and share one guest link on WhatsApp.

ReadThis is a very new, well-funded-looking but small operation. The engineering and illustration quality is far above a typical Indian e-card app, and the business is in its launch week.

Features

Ceremonies supported

  • Ring Ceremony, Tilak, Mehndi, Sangeet, Haldi, Cocktail Party, Bachelor Party, Baraat Aagman, Wedding, Reception
  • Gujarati extras on Calendar: Mandap Muhurat, Mameru
  • Each ceremony has an illustrated motif (mehndi hand, dholak, wine glass, turmeric and so on)
  • "Bride first" or "Groom first" ordering; groom-first adds Tilak and Baraat
  • Code has a ritual region fixed to "north-indian"; a Christian/chapel set is stubbed but switched off

Guest experience

  • Google Maps "Directions" per venue
  • "Add to calendar" per event (times labelled IST)
  • Background music with mute button; tap to start
  • Parents' names as "D/o" and "S/o" lines
  • Travel legs between venues (auto, car, plane) with distance and time
  • QR code of the invitation can be saved

RSVP

  • One form for all ceremonies: attending or can't attend, per event
  • Adults and children counters per event (up to 20 each)
  • Lead guest name, optional phone, optional note (600 characters)
  • Cloudflare Turnstile bot check
  • Host gets email updates at most once an hour
  • Host can download all replies as a spreadsheet
  • Thank-you screen: "Planning your own wedding? Create your own card"

Media

  • Two portraits (bride, groom) or a couple photo; JPG, PNG or WebP, up to 12 MB and 32 megapixels, with crop
  • 40+ licensed tracks, 12 of them tagged Indian (Bhangra, Festive Lights, India Moods and more)
  • Own song upload: MP3 or M4A up to 10 MB, trim start and end (minimum 5 seconds), with a rights tick-box that is logged
  • Venue autocomplete through Ola Maps

Customer journey, step by step

  1. DiscoverHomepage autoplays the seven designs inside phone frames. "Personalise this design" or "Open live demo".
  2. "Who's getting married?"Bride's name, groom's name, wedding date ("Not final yet? Pick your best guess"). No signup, no email, no phone. Draft is kept in the browser.
  3. Watermarked previewThe real animation plays with their names in seconds, overlaid with "CARDCRAFT · PREVIEW". Switching designs keeps the names.
  4. CheckoutRazorpay checkout in rupees (UPI, cards, netbanking, wallets as Razorpay offers). CardCraft asks for no contact details; Razorpay may pass on the email or phone typed there.
  5. Link goes live instantlyThe clean guest link works the moment payment is verified, but shows placeholder portraits and "Details coming soon", and RSVP stays off.
  6. Optional contact verifyWhatsApp number or email, confirmed by one-time code. Used for the link, reminders to finish the card and a "ready" note. Reply STOP ends WhatsApp messages.
  7. Fill in detailsPhotos, ceremonies, dates, venues, notes, music. Save as often as needed; guests keep seeing the earlier version.
  8. Submit final card onceFree, one time only, and it locks the card. No edits after this.
  9. ShareGuest link at cardcraft.date/i/<slug> goes out on WhatsApp. Private management link at /manage/<token>, protected by OTP on other devices.
  10. After the weddingFeedback email about 2 hours after finishing and the morning after the last ceremony ("If you tell us we may quote you"). Card deleted 6 months after the last ceremony, with a warning a week before.

ReadCharging before collecting details is a deliberate conversion trick: the couple pays at peak excitement, right after seeing their own names animate. The one-time final submit cuts support load from endless edits.

How the money works

  • ReadOne-time payment per wedding; no subscription.
  • ReadCost per order is close to zero: hosting is on Cloudflare (cheap static and edge hosting), and Razorpay's standard fee is about 2%, roughly ₹10 on ₹499. Gross margin is likely above 90% before ads and GST.
  • ReadThe ₹1,999 "list" price is an anchor to make ₹499 feel like 75% off.
  • ReadThe growth engine is the guest page itself. Every invite carries a "Made with love · CardCraft" logo and an end screen asking "Planning your own wedding?", tagged utm_source=guest. One wedding with 300 guests gives 300 free ad views to people in wedding season.
  • FactLicence terms ban reselling: "You may not resell the design, use it as a template for other couples, or offer CardCraft invitations as your own product."

Delivery and policies

What the customer gets

  • A hosted web page (guest link) on cardcraft.date
  • No printed card, no downloadable video (yet), no PDF
  • Unlisted link: anyone with the URL can open it; marked not to be indexed by Google
  • Live until 6 months after the last ceremony

Refunds

  • Only if payment was captured in error or the link cannot be published
  • Changing your mind after publication is not a refund reason
  • Refunds go back through Razorpay and revoke the page
  • Liability capped at the amount paid

Security and data

  • OTP codes expire in 10 minutes, 5 attempts, single use, 24-hour session
  • Written against India's DPDP Act 2023; data requests answered within 30 days
  • Shares data with Razorpay, Cloudflare, Resend (email), WhatsApp/Meta, Google Analytics, Meta Pixel, Ola Maps
  • Keeps a record of how people use the builder, including typed field content

Visual language

  • Hand-painted, storybook illustration rather than stock templates or photos
  • Indian craft cues: zari embroidery borders, peacocks, marigold and rose petals, diyas, lanterns, wax seals, gold leaf, mehndi and haldi motifs
  • Phone-first: every design is framed at 9:16, with 44 px touch targets and safe-area padding
  • Motion everywhere (page turns, falling petals, flying doves, swaying portraits), but it switches to quiet motion when the phone asks for reduced motion
  • Transparent video for birds and doves (HEVC .mov for iPhone, WebM for Android)
  • Copy is soft and literary: "A story waiting to be opened.", "Seven little worlds for your wedding.", "Two names and a date. See your invitation in seconds."
  • Watermark on unpaid previews: "CARDCRAFT · PREVIEW"

Status and risks

Launch week. The company is about five days old, the legal pages are dated 29 October 2026, and on 1 November the builder still showed a "Test Mode · Razorpay Checkout" badge. Real payments may not be switched on yet.

  • No social proof: no reviews, testimonials, order counts or real couples anywhere on the site.
  • Email-only support: no phone or WhatsApp number for a high-emotion, deadline-driven purchase.
  • English only: no Hindi, Punjabi, Gujarati or other wording, even though family elders are a big part of the guest list.
  • North Indian Hindu rituals only: no Sikh (Anand Karaj, Roka, Chunni, Jaggo, Milni), Muslim (Nikah, Walima), South Indian, Bengali or Christian sets yet.
  • Music copyright exposure: the library includes a "Kala Chashma" instrumental (a Bollywood song) as Tale's default track.
  • Hidden price: the price only appears at checkout, which loses comparison shoppers.
  • Strict policy: one final submit, no edits after locking, no change-of-mind refunds.

SWOT

Strengths

  • Best-in-class illustration and animation
  • Real multi-event Indian wedding fit
  • See your names before paying
  • ₹499 is an impulse price
  • RSVP with head counts per function
  • Near-zero delivery cost

Weaknesses

  • No trust signals or human support
  • English only, limited communities
  • Hosted link only, no print or video yet
  • Brand-new company, no track record

Opportunities

  • Video add-on at ₹999 (already coded)
  • Bundles, regional languages and rituals
  • Other events: engagements, birthdays, griha pravesh
  • Viral spread through every guest page

Threats

  • Cheap Canva and WhatsApp-video makers
  • Local designers offering done-for-you
  • Music copyright claims
  • Easy for competitors to copy the format

What it means for Nazakat Telecom

You cannot resell CardCraft as your own. Their licence forbids offering CardCraft invitations as your product or making them for other couples. Any partnership would need their written agreement.

Three realistic routes

  1. Partner with them. They are days old, have no reseller or affiliate programme and no offline presence. A shop that sees wedding families every day is a channel they lack. Write to hello@cardcraft.date and propose a referral fee per paid invite or an assisted-sale arrangement.
  2. Run an assisted service legally. At your counter, help a family make their own CardCraft invite on their own phone and pay with their own UPI, and charge a separate service fee for setup, photo editing and wording. Read their terms closely first or get their consent; the safest version is with their permission.
  3. Build your own local offer. Use their gaps: Punjabi and Hindi wording, Sikh ceremonies (Roka, Chunni, Jaggo, Anand Karaj, Milni, Doli), a done-for-you service, a visible price and a WhatsApp helpline. Sell it bundled with SIMs, recharges or accessories in wedding season.

Benchmarks to use

  • Do-it-yourself online price to beat or match: ₹499
  • Premium add-on benchmark: ₹999 for an invitation video
  • Features customers now expect: multiple events in one link, map directions, RSVP with head counts, music, couple photos
